⚖️ Know Your Rights: Simple Guides

Tap a topic to open it. These are general guides in plain English, not legal advice.

🏪 Market fees and levies: what you should pay

Local councils set market fees. You should only pay the council's official rate, and you have the right to an official receipt every time.

  • Ask for the receipt and keep it. It also proves your business is real.
  • Ask the council office or market master for the written list of fees.
  • If someone asks for extra money with no receipt, do not pay. Report it to the council office or the Anti-Corruption Commission.
  • Take a trusted friend or association member with you if you feel unsafe.

Rates differ by council. Always confirm with your own council.

👩 Women in business: funding and maternity

Government empowerment funding through CEEC prioritises women, youth and people with disabilities. It is a loan that must be repaid, not a free grant. See the CEEC guide below.

  • Maternity: the Employment Code gives employed women maternity leave. If you employ women, you must respect it.
  • If you run your own shop, there is no confirmed government maternity payment for shop owners that this app knows of. Plan ahead: save a small buffer, and ask a trusted helper to cover the shop.
  • Women's groups and chilimba savings groups can help you borrow together and share risk.

Ask CEEC or your council about any new women's programmes.

🧑‍🌾 Youth empowerment: CEEC and CDF

CEEC (Citizens Economic Empowerment Commission) gives empowerment loans, with priority for youth, women and people with disabilities. Its funds are loans that revolve, so repayment keeps the fund going for others.

  • CEEC announced new loan products in 2026 and earlier pending applications were cancelled. Check the current rules and dates at www.ceec.org.zm or the nearest CEEC office.
  • Application forms should not need a fee. Be careful of anyone who asks you to pay to apply.
  • CDF (Constituency Development Fund): ask your ward or constituency office how groups, cooperatives and youth can apply.
  • Keep your PACRA registration, a simple business plan and your monthly records ready. The Lender Report in the Loan Score screen can help.

Amounts, ages and rules change. Confirm with the office first.

♿ People with disabilities: tax help

If you are registered with the Zambia Agency for Persons with Disabilities (ZAPD), you may get these:

  • A yearly income tax credit. ZRA has quoted K600 a month (K7,200 a year). Ask ZRA to confirm the current figure.
  • No VAT, or zero-rated VAT, on items made for people with disabilities.
  • Duty relief on a modified vehicle for a registered person, once every five years.
  • If you employ a person with a disability, your business may claim a tax deduction. The 2026 Budget raised it from K2,000 to K2,500.

Register with ZAPD first, then ask ZRA how to claim. Never lend your disability card to anyone. Misuse is a crime.

🧾 Your basic rights as a small trader
  • You may ask any official to show ID and say which law or by-law they are using.
  • You have the right to a receipt for every fee, fine or levy you pay.
  • Turnover tax is 5% of sales, for businesses selling up to K5,000,000 a year. You may not need to pay VAT too.
  • Keep your monthly records. They protect you in a dispute and help you get loans.

📚 Zambian Business Rules in Simple English

Know your rights and protect your personal property as a Zambian business owner.

⚖️ Protecting Your Personal House & Savings (Limited Company)

When you register as a Limited Company with PACRA, your business becomes separate from you personally. If the shop runs into debt, suppliers cannot take your family house or personal savings.

🛡️ Getting Time to Recover When Cash Is Tight

Under Zambian insolvency law, struggling businesses can apply for "Corporate Rescue"—which pauses debt collection temporarily so you can reorganize and save your shop instead of closing down.

🤝 Which Business Type Fits Your Shop?

• Sole Trader: Fastest to start. You own everything alone, but you are personally responsible for all shop debts.
• Partnership: Two or more people running a shop together. Always sign a written agreement.
• Limited Company: Best for growing shops, applying for bank/CEEC loans, and supplying big companies.

New VAT Rate
